# Spice and Wolf, Volume 4

## Synopsis

Having learned from the chronicler in Kumersun (who was secretly a giant bird spirit) that there is a friar up north who collected pagan tales and might be able to tell them more about Holo and Yoitsu, Lawrence and Holo head north. They first stop in at the town of Enberch, where after asking around, a local wheat seller named Riendott directs them to a small village further up the road called Tereo.

Arriving in Tereo, the two meet a young miller named Evan, as well as a young priest named Elsa. As they ask around, Lawrence and Holo find that the villagers are reluctant to speak about the friar and his abbey, and also learn that Tereo's relationship with Enberch is strained. The tensions originate from Tereo's worship of a pagan god--a large serpent known as Lord Trugeo--which is heavily frowned upon by the Church in Enberch. It's also somewhat antagonizing, due to the presence of a church (run by Elsa) in Tereo. 

Tereo is able to get away with worshipping Truyeo, however, because Elsa's predecessor, Father Franz, was very well-connected and able to stave off accusations of heresy. To further complicate matters, Father Franz was able to negotiate an extremely favorable wheat-selling contract between Tereo and Enberch that allowed Tereo to essentially name its price when it came to selling the harvest. Father Franz has passed away however, and Elsa has been unable to garner any of the trust and support that he possessed.

In the process of learning all of this, Lawrence comes to the realization that the friar he was told of in Kumersun was likely Father Franz, and the church in Tereo was his "abbey." As such, he and Holo confront Elsa and convince her to give them access to Father Franz's vast records. It turns out that despite being a man of God, Father Franz was a thorough researcher of pagan legends, a fact that causes Elsa to question her own beliefs.

The tenuous political and religious situation in Tereo makes Lawrence uneasy, but Father Franz's collection is quite extensive, so he and Holo decide to stay for some time to read through it and search for any leads on Yoitsu's fate or whereabouts. However, while they are reading through these records, the Tereo-Enberch situation falls apart; a messenger arrives, informing the villagers that someone has died from the wehat Tereo sold Enberch. Consequently, a messenger will in all likelihood arrive at dawn demanding that Tereo refund the entire harvest.

For the villagers of Tereo, who have grown complacent off of the comfortable agreement Father Franz secured for them, this is a crisis. Furthermore, Lawrence suspects that he and Holo will be the first ones on the chopping block, as travelers are always the first to be suspected in times of trouble, and their arrival in Tereo coincided closely with this incident. He also realizes that even if he and Holo were to escape, the villagers would only focus their anger on Evan and Elsa instead, who have also been the subject of constant distrust.

Unable to determine a solution to Tereo's problem, Lawrence decides that the only way he and Holo will get out of this alive is to cut their losses and flee, using Holo's wolf form. They offer to bring Elsa and Evan with them, but before they can fully leave the region, the priest and miller decide instead to return to Tereo. Elsa wants to offer her assistance, despite knowing full she would likely just be scorned and executed, and Evan wants to protect her. Once again Holo with Holo however, Lawrence finally comes up with a plan to turn the situation around. They catch up with Elsa and Evan, and the four sneak back into Tereo.

In Tereo, a procession from Enberch has arrived, consisting of wagons containing Tereo's wheat to be returned, Riendott, and, to Lawrence's mild surprise, Bishop Van. Lawrence surmises that Enberch plans to use this opportunity to not only dismantle Father Franz's contract and subjugate Tereo financially, but to accuse the village of heresy and force them to convert fully. 

Lawrence, Holo, Elsa, and Evan reveal themselves in the village square, to everyone's surprise. Having reconciled her own beliefs about God and Truyeo, Elsa confronts Bishop Van, and makes the bold claim that Lord Truyeo is one of God's creations. This would thus prevent the Church from denying Truyeo's existence. She proves this with a miracle: Holo discreetly causes the wheat in the wagons to grow instantanteously, stunning everyone except for Lawrence and the others. Unable to deny Elsa's proclamation, Bishop Van agrees to recognize Truyeo as legitimate.

With the religious problem solved, Lawrence then confronts Riendott about the returned wheat. Coming to the conclusion that Riendott set Lawrence and Holo up as scapegoats, and that the report of someone dying from the wheat was likely a myth, Lawrence pressures Riendott into accepting a reduced refund on the harvest, knowing that the merchant would prefer to avoid having his treachery exposed, particularly in front of the villagers. Lawrence also persuades Bishop Van to allow Tereo to sell wheat under his name. However, Lawrence also dismantles Father Frans's contract. Though the villagers are initially dismayed and outraged by this, Lawrence reasons that the contract was a source of resentment for Enberch, and that an incident like this could happen again in the future as long as it remained in place.

Instead, Lawrence teaches the villagers how to make cookies, which he noticed were completely unheard of in the northern lands. Though the cookies sell extremely well due to their uniqueness, Lawrence knows that others will soon copy Tereo. However, he hopes that they'll use this head-start to become self-sustaining.

Elsa finally gains acceptance from the village, and Evan steps up to handle negotiations between Enberch and Tereo. Lawrence and Holo thus pack up and set out for Lenos, a town where legends of Holo are said to still exist today.
